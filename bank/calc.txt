@ calc e calc-e-002
T: Derivative of the sigmoid
S: derivatives
Q: For $\sigma(x)=\dfrac{1}{1+e^{-x}}$, $\sigma'(x)$ equals:
A: $\sigma(x)(1-\sigma(x))$
B: $\sigma(x)^2$
C: $1-\sigma(x)$
D: $\sigma(x)(1+\sigma(x))$
ANS: A
EXP: $\sigma'(x)=\dfrac{e^{-x}}{(1+e^{-x})^2}=\dfrac{1}{1+e^{-x}}\cdot\dfrac{e^{-x}}{1+e^{-x}}=\sigma(x)(1-\sigma(x))$.
Its maximum is $\tfrac14$ at $x=0$, one reason deep sigmoid networks suffer vanishing gradients.

@ calc e calc-e-006
T: Minimum of a quadratic
S: single-var-optimization
Q: What is the minimum value of $f(x)=x^2-4x+7$?
A: $7$
B: $2$
C: $3$
D: $-3$
ANS: C
EXP: $f'(x)=2x-4=0\Rightarrow x=2$, and $f''=2>0$ so it is a minimum. $f(2)=4-8+7=3$.
Completing the square gives the same: $(x-2)^2+3$. ($2$ is where the minimum occurs, not its value.)

@ calc e calc-e-010

S: maxima-minima
Q: Where does $f(x)=x^3-3x$ have a local maximum?
A: $x=1$
B: $x=-1$
C: $x=0$
D: $x=\sqrt3$
ANS: B

@ calc m calc-m-001
T: Gradient of a quadratic form
S: gradients-multivariable
Q: For a general (not necessarily symmetric) square matrix $A$, $\nabla_x\left(x^TAx\right)=$
A: $Ax$
B: $2Ax$
C: $(A+A^T)x$
D: $x^TA$
ANS: C
EXP: Write $x^TAx=\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}x_ix_j$; then $\partial/\partial x_k=\sum_jA_{kj}x_j+\sum_iA_{ik}x_i=(Ax+A^Tx)_k$.
It reduces to $2Ax$ only when $A$ is symmetric (e.g. the least squares Hessian $X^TX$).


@ calc m calc-m-002
T: Classifying a critical point with the Hessian
S: maxima-minima
Q: $f(x,y)=x^2+3xy+y^2$ has a critical point at the origin. It is a:
A: local minimum
B: local maximum
C: saddle point
D: cannot be determined
ANS: C
EXP: Hessian $H=\begin{pmatrix}2&3\\3&2\end{pmatrix}$, $\det H=4-9=-5<0$, so the eigenvalues have opposite signs ($5$ and $-1$).
Indefinite Hessian means a saddle: along $y=x$, $f=5x^2$ rises; along $y=-x$, $f=-x^2$ falls.


@ calc m calc-m-003
T: Second-order Taylor expansion
S: taylor-series
Q: The second-order Taylor approximation of $\ln(1+x)$ around $x=0$ is:
A: $x-\dfrac{x^2}{2}$
B: $x+\dfrac{x^2}{2}$
C: $1+x-\dfrac{x^2}{2}$
D: $x-x^2$
ANS: A
EXP: $f(0)=0$, $f'(x)=\tfrac1{1+x}\Rightarrow f'(0)=1$, $f''(x)=-\tfrac1{(1+x)^2}\Rightarrow f''(0)=-1$.
$f\approx0+x+\tfrac{-1}{2}x^2=x-\tfrac{x^2}{2}$.


@ calc m calc-m-004

B: $\frac12$
C: $\infty$
D: $e$
ANS: A

@ calc m calc-m-005

S: constrained-optimization

@ calc m calc-m-007
T: Gradient of softmax cross-entropy
S: gradients-multivariable
Q: With logits $z$, $p=\operatorname{softmax}(z)$ and one-hot label $y$, the loss is $L=-\sum_iy_i\log p_i$. What is $\partial L/\partial z$?
A: $p-y$
B: $y-p$
C: $p(1-p)$
D: $-y/p$
ANS: A
EXP: $\log p_i=z_i-\log\sum_je^{z_j}$, so $\partial\log p_i/\partial z_k=\delta_{ik}-p_k$.
$\partial L/\partial z_k=-\sum_iy_i(\delta_{ik}-p_k)=-y_k+p_k\sum_iy_i=p_k-y_k$ (since $\sum y_i=1$).
$-y/p$ is the gradient with respect to $p$, not $z$.


@ calc m calc-m-008
T: Directional derivative
S: gradients-multivariable
Q: $f(x,y)=x^2+y^2$. What is the directional derivative at $(1,1)$ in the direction of $(3,4)$?
A: $\frac{14}{5}$
B: $4$
C: $2\sqrt2$
D: $14$
ANS: A
EXP: $\nabla f=(2x,2y)=(2,2)$. Unit direction $u=(3,4)/5$.
$D_uf=\nabla f\cdot u=\tfrac{6+8}{5}=\tfrac{14}{5}$. Forgetting to normalise gives $14$; $2\sqrt2=\|\nabla f\|$ is the maximum rate.


@ calc m calc-m-009
T: Stable learning rate on a quadratic
S: gradient-descent
Q: Gradient descent on $f(x)=2x^2$ with a fixed step $\eta>0$ converges from any start if and only if:
A: $\eta<0.25$
B: $\eta<0.5$
C: $\eta<1$
D: $\eta<2$
ANS: B
EXP: $f'(x)=4x$, so $x_{t+1}=x_t-4\eta x_t=(1-4\eta)x_t$. Convergence needs $|1-4\eta|<1\Rightarrow0<\eta<\tfrac12$.
In general $\eta<2/L$, where $L$ is the largest curvature. $\eta=0.25$ is the one-step optimal rate, not the limit.


@ calc m calc-m-010
T: Spotting a non-convex function
S: convexity
Q: Which function is NOT convex on its domain?
A: $e^x$
B: $-\log x\ (x>0)$
C: $\max(0,x)$
D: $\sqrt x\ (x\ge0)$
ANS: D
EXP: $(\sqrt x)''=-\tfrac14x^{-3/2}<0$, so $\sqrt x$ is concave, not convex.
$e^x$ and $-\log x$ have positive second derivatives, and ReLU is a maximum of linear functions, so it is convex.


@ calc h calc-h-001
T: Two Newton steps for √2
S: single-var-optimization
Q: Apply Newton's method to $f(x)=x^2-2$ starting at $x_0=1$. What is $x_2$?
A: $\frac32$
B: $\frac{17}{12}$
C: $\frac75$
D: $\frac{577}{408}$
ANS: B
EXP: Newton: $x_{n+1}=x_n-\dfrac{x_n^2-2}{2x_n}=\tfrac12\left(x_n+\tfrac2{x_n}\right)$.
$x_1=\tfrac12(1+2)=\tfrac32$. $x_2=\tfrac12\left(\tfrac32+\tfrac43\right)=\tfrac{17}{12}\approx1.4167$.
$\tfrac{577}{408}$ is $x_3$, showing quadratic convergence (correct digits roughly double each step).


@ calc h calc-h-002
T: Gradient of log-determinant
S: gradients-multivariable
Q: For an invertible matrix $X$ with $\det X>0$, $\nabla_X\log\det X=$
A: $X^{-T}$
B: $X$
C: $\det(X)\,X^{-1}$
D: $X^T$
ANS: A
EXP: Jacobi's formula: $d\log\det X=\operatorname{tr}(X^{-1}dX)=\langle X^{-T},dX\rangle$, so the gradient is $(X^{-1})^T$.
For symmetric $X$ (e.g. a covariance in a Gaussian log-likelihood) it is $X^{-1}$.


@ calc h calc-h-003
T: Gaussian integral
S: integrals
Q: $\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^2/2}\,dx=$
A: $\sqrt\pi$
B: $\sqrt{2\pi}$
C: $2\pi$
D: $1$
ANS: B
EXP: $I^2=\iint e^{-(x^2+y^2)/2}dx\,dy=\int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_0^\infty e^{-r^2/2}r\,dr\,d\theta=2\pi$, so $I=\sqrt{2\pi}$.
That is why the normal density carries the factor $\tfrac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}$. ($\sqrt\pi$ is for $e^{-x^2}$.)

@ calc h calc-h-005
T: Constrained minimisation (KKT)
S: constrained-optimization
Q: Minimise $x^2+y^2$ subject to $x+2y\ge5$. Minimum value and minimiser:
A: $5$ at $(1,2)$
B: $\sqrt5$ at $(1,2)$
C: $\frac{25}{4}$ at $(0,\frac52)$
D: $25$ at $(5,0)$
ANS: A
EXP: The unconstrained minimum $(0,0)$ violates the constraint, so it is active: $x+2y=5$.
KKT: $(2x,2y)=\mu(1,2)$ with $\mu\ge0\Rightarrow y=2x$, so $5x=5$, $x=1$, $y=2$, $\mu=2>0$.
Value $=1+4=5$. ($\sqrt5$ is the distance; the objective is the squared distance.)


@ calc h calc-h-006
T: Gradient descent and the condition number
S: gradient-descent
Q: Gradient descent on a quadratic whose Hessian has eigenvalues $1$ and $100$, using the best fixed step $\eta=\frac{2}{L+\mu}$. The error contracts each step by a factor of:
A: $\frac{99}{101}$
B: $\frac{1}{100}$
C: $\frac{99}{100}$
D: $\frac12$
ANS: A
EXP: With $\eta=\tfrac2{\mu+L}=\tfrac2{101}$, each eigen-direction scales by $|1-\eta\lambda|$: $|1-\tfrac2{101}|=\tfrac{99}{101}$ and $|1-\tfrac{200}{101}|=\tfrac{99}{101}$.
In general the factor is $\tfrac{\kappa-1}{\kappa+1}$ with $\kappa=L/\mu=100$. Large $\kappa$ makes convergence slow, which motivates feature scaling, momentum and Adam.

@ calc h calc-h-008
T: Hessian of log-sum-exp
S: convexity
Q: Let $f(z)=\log\sum_ie^{z_i}$ and $p=\operatorname{softmax}(z)$. The Hessian $\nabla^2f(z)$ is:
A: $\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^T$
B: $pp^T$
C: $\operatorname{diag}(p)$
D: $I-pp^T$
ANS: A
EXP: $\nabla f=p$. $\partial p_i/\partial z_j=p_i(\delta_{ij}-p_j)$, so $H=\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^T$.
It is PSD ($v^THv=\operatorname{Var}_p(v)\ge0$), which proves log-sum-exp is convex and so is the softmax cross-entropy loss in the logits.


@ calc h calc-h-009
T: Maximise xy² on a line
S: constrained-optimization
Q: Maximise $f(x,y)=xy^2$ subject to $x+y=3$, $x,y\ge0$.
A: $4$
B: $\frac{27}{8}$
C: $0$
D: $9$
ANS: A
EXP: Substitute $x=3-y$: $g(y)=(3-y)y^2=3y^2-y^3$. $g'(y)=6y-3y^2=0\Rightarrow y=2$ (or $0$).
$x=1$, $f=1\cdot4=4$. The symmetric guess $x=y=1.5$ gives only $\tfrac{27}{8}$.


@ calc h calc-h-010
T: Smoothness constant of logistic loss
S: gradient-descent
Q: What is the smallest $L$ such that $f(x)=\log(1+e^x)$ has an $L$-Lipschitz derivative?
A: $1$
B: $\frac12$
C: $\frac14$
D: $\infty$
ANS: C
EXP: $f'=\sigma(x)$ and $f''=\sigma(1-\sigma)$, which peaks at $\tfrac14$ when $\sigma=\tfrac12$ ($x=0$).
So $L=\sup|f''|=\tfrac14$, and the safe GD step is $\eta\le1/L=4$ (for one sample with a unit feature).
