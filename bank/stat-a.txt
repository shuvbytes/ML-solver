S: sampling-distributions
Q: $Z\sim N(0,1)$ and $V\sim\chi^2_k$ are independent. What is the distribution of $T=\dfrac{Z}{\sqrt{V/k}}$?

@ stat e stat-e-022
T: Which linear estimators are unbiased
S: bias-variance
TYPE: msq
Q: $X_1,X_2$ are iid with mean $\mu$. Which of the following are unbiased estimators of $\mu$? More than one option may be correct.
A: $X_1$
B: $\dfrac{X_1+X_2}{2}$
C: $2X_1-X_2$
D: $\dfrac{X_1+X_2}{3}$
ANS: A,B,C
EXP: By linearity, $E[aX_1+bX_2]=(a+b)\mu$, so the estimator is unbiased exactly when the weights sum to 1.
$X_1$: $1$. $\frac{X_1+X_2}{2}$: $\frac12+\frac12=1$. $2X_1-X_2$: $2-1=1$. All unbiased.

B: bias and variance both decrease
C: bias decreases and variance increases
D: bias and variance both increase

Asymptotically $\operatorname{Var}(\hat\lambda)\approx\dfrac{1}{nI(\lambda)}=\dfrac{\lambda^2}{n}$, so $\text{SE}\approx\dfrac{\hat\lambda}{\sqrt n}=\dfrac{2}{10}=0.2$.
$0.04$ is the variance, not the SE. $0.05=\bar x/\sqrt n$ is the SE of $\bar x$, the estimate of the mean $1/\lambda$, not of $\lambda$.

T: Allele frequency by maximum likelihood
S: estimation-mle
Q: Under Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium, genotypes AA, Aa, aa have probabilities $\theta^2$, $2\theta(1-\theta)$, $(1-\theta)^2$. A sample of 100 individuals has 50 AA, 40 Aa and 10 aa. What is the MLE of $\theta$?

@ stat h stat-h-029
T: MSE-optimal shrinkage of the sample mean
S: bias-variance
TYPE: nat
TOL: 0.01
Q: $X_1,\dots,X_4$ are iid with mean $\mu=2$ and variance $\sigma^2=4$. Among estimators of the form $c\bar X$, which $c$ minimises the MSE for estimating $\mu$? Give your answer to 2 decimal places.
ANS: 0.8
EXP: $E[c\bar X]=c\mu$ and $\operatorname{Var}(c\bar X)=c^2\sigma^2/n$, so $\text{MSE}(c)=c^2\dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}+(c-1)^2\mu^2$.
Setting the derivative to 0: $c\dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}+(c-1)\mu^2=0\Rightarrow c^*=\dfrac{\mu^2}{\mu^2+\sigma^2/n}=\dfrac{4}{4+1}=0.8$.
MSE at $c=0.8$: $0.64+0.16=0.8$, below the unbiased choice $c=1$ (MSE $1$). Shrinking toward 0 trades a little bias for a larger cut in variance, the idea behind ridge regression. (In practice $c^*$ depends on the unknown $\mu$, so it has to be estimated.)

T: MSE of the normal-variance MLE
S: bias-variance
Q: $X_1,\dots,X_5$ are iid $N(\mu,1)$. What is the MSE of the MLE $\hat\sigma^2=\frac1n\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2$ as an estimator of $\sigma^2=1$?

B: $0.32$
C: $0.36$
D: $0.04$
ANS: C

@ stat h stat-h-031
T: Unbiasedness of nonlinear estimators
S: bias-variance
TYPE: msq
Q: $X_1,\dots,X_n$ are iid with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2>0$, $n\ge2$. $S^2$ is the sample variance with denominator $n-1$. Which of the following are true? More than one option may be correct.
A: $S^2$ is unbiased for $\sigma^2$.
B: $S$ is unbiased for $\sigma$.
C: $\bar X^2$ is unbiased for $\mu^2$.
D: $\bar X^2-\dfrac{S^2}{n}$ is unbiased for $\mu^2$.
ANS: A,D
EXP: $E[S^2]=\sigma^2$ for any distribution with finite variance. True.
$S=\sqrt{S^2}$ and $\sqrt{\cdot}$ is strictly concave, so by Jensen $E[S]<\sqrt{E[S^2]}=\sigma$ (strict since $S^2$ is not constant). False.
$E[\bar X^2]=\operatorname{Var}(\bar X)+\mu^2=\mu^2+\dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}$, biased upward. False.
$E\left[\bar X^2-\tfrac{S^2}{n}\right]=\mu^2+\tfrac{\sigma^2}{n}-\tfrac{\sigma^2}{n}=\mu^2$. True.
Unbiasedness does not survive nonlinear transformations, unlike the invariance property of MLEs.


@ stat h stat-h-032
T: Test error of k-nearest-neighbour regression
S: bias-variance
TYPE: nat
TOL: 0.01
Q: Data follow $Y=f(x)+\varepsilon$ with $\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon)=1$, and the training inputs are fixed. A $k$-NN regressor with $k=4$ predicts at $x_0$ by averaging the training $Y$ values of its 4 nearest neighbours. The average of $f$ at those neighbours differs from $f(x_0)$ by $0.5$. What is the expected squared error $E\big[(Y_0-\hat f(x_0))^2\big]$ for a new response $Y_0$ at $x_0$? Give your answer to 2 decimal places.
ANS: 1.5

@ stat h stat-h-038
T: Bonferroni critical value for joint intervals
S: confidence-intervals
Q: You report two-sided $z$-intervals for 3 different means and want all three to cover their true means simultaneously with probability at least 95%. Using the Bonferroni correction, which critical value should each interval use? $z_{0.025}=1.96$, $z_{0.0167}=2.128$, $z_{0.00833}=2.394$, $z_{0.005}=2.576$.
A: $2.394$
B: $1.96$
C: $2.128$
D: $2.576$
ANS: A
EXP: Bonferroni: $P(\text{some interval misses})\le\sum P(\text{miss}_j)$. Giving each interval miss probability $0.05/3$ keeps the total at most $0.05$.

@ stat h stat-h-039

A: The 95% CI for the difference is $(-0.92,\ 6.92)$, so the difference is not significant at 5%.
B: The 95% CI for the difference is $(0.23,\ 5.77)$, so the difference is significant at 5%.

ANS: B
EXP: $\text{SE}(\bar X_2-\bar X_1)=\sqrt{1^2+1^2}=\sqrt2\approx1.414$, so the interval is $3\pm1.96\cdot1.414=3\pm2.77=(0.23,\ 5.77)$. It excludes 0, so the difference is significant.

@ stat h stat-h-040
T: Upper bound when no events are observed
S: confidence-intervals
Q: A model makes $0$ critical errors in $300$ independent test cases. What is an approximate 95% one-sided upper confidence bound for its true error rate $p$? (Use the exact condition $(1-p)^{300}=0.05$ and $\ln0.05\approx-3$.)
A: $0$
B: $0.0033$
C: $0.0065$
D: $0.01$
ANS: D
EXP: The upper bound is the largest $p$ for which seeing 0 errors still has probability at least $0.05$: $(1-p)^{300}=0.05$.
Taking logs: $300\ln(1-p)=\ln0.05\approx-3$, and $\ln(1-p)\approx-p$, so $p\approx\frac{3}{300}=0.01$ (exactly $0.00994$). This is the "rule of three".
The Wald interval $\hat p\pm1.96\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)/n}$ collapses to $[0,0]$ because $\hat p=0$, absurdly claiming certainty. $0.0033=1/300$ is a point-estimate-style guess, not a 95% bound.
