B: $-2$

@ la e la-e-013
T: Cosine similarity of two vectors
S: vectors-norms
TYPE: nat
TOL: 0.01
Q: Find the cosine similarity $\dfrac{u\cdot v}{\|u\|\,\|v\|}$ of $u=(1,2,2)$ and $v=(2,1,2)$. Give your answer to 2 decimal places.
ANS: 0.89

@ la e la-e-024

S: linear-systems

A: no solution
B: exactly one solution
C: infinitely many solutions
D: exactly two solutions
ANS: A
EXP: By the Rouché–Capelli criterion, $Ax=b$ is consistent if and only if $\operatorname{rank}(A)=\operatorname{rank}([A\mid b])$.
Here the ranks differ: adding $b$ raised the rank, so $b$ is not in the column space of $A$. There is no solution.

The singular $\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$ is the trap: it does factor, $L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$, $U=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$. A zero in the last pivot is allowed.
[[C]] and [[D]] have nonzero first pivots ($1$ and $2$), so elimination goes through.

T: Singular values from eigenvalues of A^T A
S: svd-pca
Q: For a matrix $A$, the eigenvalues of $A^TA$ are $16$ and $9$. What are the singular values of $A$?
A: $4$ and $3$
B: $16$ and $9$
C: $256$ and $81$
D: $8$ and $4.5$
ANS: A
EXP: The singular values of $A$ are the square roots of the eigenvalues of $A^TA$: $\sigma_i=\sqrt{\lambda_i(A^TA)}$.
So $\sigma=\sqrt{16}=4$ and $\sqrt9=3$.
Taking the eigenvalues of $A^TA$ themselves as singular values ($16$, $9$) is the usual slip. They are $\sigma_i^2$.


@ la e la-e-048
T: Variance explained by two principal components
S: svd-pca
TYPE: nat
TOL: 0
Q: The covariance matrix of a 4-feature dataset has eigenvalues $5,3,1,1$. What percentage of the total variance is explained by the first two principal components? Enter a number (e.g. 50 for 50%).
ANS: 80
EXP: Each principal component's variance is its eigenvalue, and the total variance is the trace (sum of eigenvalues): $5+3+1+1=10$.
The first two PCs explain $\frac{5+3}{10}=0.8$, i.e. $80\%$.
Dividing by the number of features (4) instead of the total variance is a common mistake. This ratio is what you look at in a scree plot to choose how many components to keep.

Q: A $4\times3$ matrix has singular values $6,\ 2,\ 0$. What is its rank?
A: $2$
B: $3$
C: $4$
D: $1$
ANS: A
EXP: The rank equals the number of nonzero singular values. Here $6$ and $2$ are nonzero, so the rank is $2$.

@ la e la-e-050
T: Direction of the first principal component
S: svd-pca
Q: For centred data, the first principal component direction is:
A: the eigenvector of the sample covariance matrix with the largest eigenvalue
B: the eigenvector of the sample covariance matrix with the smallest eigenvalue
C: the unit vector along the original feature with the largest variance
D: the unit vector in the direction of the mean of the data
ANS: A
EXP: PCA finds the unit vector $w$ maximising the projected variance $w^TSw$, where $S$ is the covariance matrix.
The maximum of $w^TSw$ over $\|w\|=1$ is the largest eigenvalue, reached at its eigenvector (the Rayleigh quotient).
The smallest eigenvalue's eigenvector is the direction of least variance. The best direction is usually a mix of features, not one original axis. For centred data the mean is $0$, so it gives no direction.
