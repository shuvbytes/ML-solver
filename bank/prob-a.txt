EXP: Odds form of Bayes: posterior odds = prior odds $\times$ likelihood ratio.
Prior odds $=\tfrac1{99}$. Each positive has likelihood ratio $\tfrac{0.9}{0.1}=9$; with conditional independence the ratios multiply: $81$.
Posterior odds $=\tfrac{81}{99}$, so $P=\dfrac{81}{81+99}=\dfrac{81}{180}=0.45$.
$\approx0.083=\tfrac1{12}$ is the posterior after one positive test. $0.81=0.9^2$ is $P(++\mid\text{condition})$, the reversed conditional. Sequential updating like this is how naive Bayes combines features.


@ prob h prob-h-024
T: Host opens a door without knowing
S: conditional-bayes
Q: Three doors hide 1 car and 2 goats. You pick door 1. The host does NOT know where the car is; he opens one of doors 2 and 3 uniformly at random, and it happens to reveal a goat. If you now switch to the other unopened door, what is your probability of winning?
A: $\frac23$
B: $\frac13$
C: $\frac12$
D: $\frac34$
ANS: C
EXP: Condition on the evidence $G$ = "the opened door shows a goat".
Car behind door 1 (prob $\tfrac13$): $P(G)=1$. Car behind door 2 or 3 (prob $\tfrac23$): $P(G)=\tfrac12$.
$P(G)=\tfrac13+\tfrac23\cdot\tfrac12=\tfrac23$.

@ prob h prob-h-025
T: Bit through two noisy channels
S: conditional-bayes
TYPE: nat

Q: A bit is sent as 1 with probability $0.6$ and as 0 with probability $0.4$. It passes through two noisy links in sequence; each link flips the bit with probability $0.1$, independently. The received bit is 1. What is the probability that a 1 was sent? Enter a decimal to 2 places.

T: Coin bias making two events independent
S: independence
Q: A coin with $P(H)=p$ is tossed twice. $A$ = first toss is heads; $B$ = both tosses show the same face. For which values of $p\in[0,1]$ are $A$ and $B$ independent?

B: $p\in\{0,\frac12,1\}$
C: $p\in\{\frac12,1\}$
D: Every $p\in[0,1]$
ANS: B
EXP: $P(A)=p$, $P(B)=p^2+(1-p)^2$, $P(A\cap B)=P(HH)=p^2$.
