D: $P(|S_n-n/2|\le10)\to1$
ANS: A,B,C
EXP: $S_n$ has mean $n/2$ and SD $\sqrt n/2$.
[[A]] True: the single most likely value still has probability $\binom n{n/2}2^{-n}\approx\sqrt{2/(\pi n)}\to0$; the mass spreads over about $\sqrt n$ values.
[[B]] True: by the CLT, $P(S_n\le n/2)\to\Phi(0)=1/2$ (from above, because of the atom at $n/2$, which vanishes by the first statement).
[[C]] True: the weak LLN, since $\operatorname{Var}(S_n/n)=\frac1{4n}\to0$.
[[D]] False: $P(|S_n-n/2|\le10)\approx P(|Z|\le20/\sqrt n)\to0$. The proportion settles down, but the count of heads drifts ever further from $n/2$ in absolute terms. Confusing the two is the gambler's fallacy.

@ prob h prob-h-072
T: Return time to a leaf of a star graph
S: markov-chains
TYPE: nat
TOL: 0
Q: A random walk on the star graph with one centre and $4$ leaves moves at each step to a uniformly random neighbour of its current vertex. Starting at a particular leaf, find the expected number of steps until it first returns to that leaf.
ANS: 8
EXP: For a random walk on a connected graph, $\pi_v=\frac{\deg v}{2|E|}$. Here $|E|=4$, so $\pi_{\text{leaf}}=\frac18$.
Kac's formula: expected return time to $v$ is $1/\pi_v=8$.
Check directly: each excursion is leaf $\to$ centre $\to$ some leaf (2 steps), and it hits our leaf with probability $\frac14$, so the expected number of excursions is $4$ and the expected time is $2\times4=8$.
Answering $4$ counts excursions, not steps. Answering $5$ takes $\pi$ uniform over the $5$ vertices, but the walk visits the centre far more often than any leaf.

@ prob h prob-h-073
T: Expected time to absorption
S: markov-chains
TYPE: nat
TOL: 0
Q: A Markov chain on $\{1,2,3\}$ has transition matrix $P=\begin{pmatrix}0.6&0.4&0\\0.3&0.2&0.5\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}$, where row $i$ is the current state and $P_{ij}=P(X_{n+1}=j\mid X_n=i)$. Starting from state $1$, find the expected number of steps until the chain reaches state $3$.
ANS: 6
EXP: First-step analysis. Let $t_i$ be the expected time to reach $3$ from $i$; $t_3=0$.
$t_1=1+0.6t_1+0.4t_2$, so $0.4t_1=1+0.4t_2$ and $t_1=2.5+t_2$.
$t_2=1+0.3t_1+0.2t_2$, so $0.8t_2=1+0.3(2.5+t_2)$, giving $0.5t_2=1.75$ and $t_2=3.5$.
$t_1=2.5+3.5=6$.
A quick guess like $1/0.5=2$ (the waiting time once in state $2$) ignores the time spent in state $1$ and the falls back from $2$ to $1$.

@ prob h prob-h-074
T: Long-run fraction for a doubly stochastic chain
S: markov-chains
Q: A Markov chain on $\{1,2,3\}$ has transition matrix $P=\begin{pmatrix}0.5&0.3&0.2\\0.2&0.5&0.3\\0.3&0.2&0.5\end{pmatrix}$, where row $i$ is the current state. In the long run, what fraction of time does the chain spend in state $3$?
A: $1/3$
B: $1/2$
C: $0.2$
D: $0.3$
ANS: A
EXP: Each column also sums to $1$ ($0.5+0.2+0.3$, $0.3+0.5+0.2$, $0.2+0.3+0.5$), so $P$ is doubly stochastic.
Then $\pi=(\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13)$ satisfies $\pi P=\pi$: each entry of $\pi P$ is $\frac13\times(\text{column sum})=\frac13$.
The chain is irreducible and aperiodic (it has self-loops), so this is the unique stationary distribution and the long-run fraction is $1/3$.
$0.5$, $0.2$ and $0.3$ are single entries of $P$, which are one-step probabilities, not long-run fractions.

@ prob h prob-h-075
T: Stationary distribution by detailed balance
S: markov-chains
Q: A Markov chain on $\{0,1,2\}$ has transition matrix $P=\begin{pmatrix}2/3&1/3&0\\1/3&1/3&1/3\\0&2/3&1/3\end{pmatrix}$, where row $i$ is the current state. In the long run, what fraction of time does it spend in state $2$?
A: $1/3$
B: $2/5$
C: $1/4$
D: $1/5$
ANS: D
EXP: The chain only moves between neighbouring states (a birth–death chain), so the stationary distribution satisfies detailed balance $\pi_iP_{i,i+1}=\pi_{i+1}P_{i+1,i}$.
$\pi_0\cdot\frac13=\pi_1\cdot\frac13\Rightarrow\pi_1=\pi_0$, and $\pi_1\cdot\frac13=\pi_2\cdot\frac23\Rightarrow\pi_2=\frac{\pi_1}2$.
Normalise: $\pi_0(1+1+\frac12)=1$, so $\pi=(\tfrac25,\tfrac25,\tfrac15)$ and $\pi_2=1/5$.
$1/3$ would need a doubly stochastic matrix, but the columns sum to $1$, $4/3$ and $2/3$. $2/5$ is $\pi_0$ or $\pi_1$. Detailed balance is also the condition MCMC samplers such as Metropolis–Hastings are built to satisfy.
